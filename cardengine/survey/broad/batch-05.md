# Broad survey batch 05: games 41-50 (Arkham Horror LCG through Hex: Shards of Fate)

## Arkham Horror: The Card Game (2016, Fantasy Flight Games) — live
- Players: asym
- Turns: full; each investigator takes a full turn (3 actions) in chosen order, then a scripted Mythos phase acts
- Resources: growing; gain 1 resource each turn, spent to play assets/events from hand
- Life: counter; health and sanity counters, defeated at 0 of either; a scenario also ends via its agenda/act decks
- Combat: other; fighting or evading an enemy is a skill test (skill + committed cards + a random token pull vs. a difficulty), not a paired attacker/defender exchange
- Responses: windows; fast and response abilities trigger off named events, no shared stack
- Board: other; an investigator's zone is a graph of connected, revealed location cards, not a fixed shape
- Card kinds: heroes (investigator), units (enemy, loosely), permanents (asset, location), spells (event); skill cards, committed to tests and never "cast," fit none
- Hook: Cooperative play against a scripted encounter deck whose enemies and scenario cards act through fixed, triggered rules rather than a second player's real choices.
- Libraries sufficient: mostly; heroes, permanents, spells and combat's keyword vocabulary map cleanly
- Missing rules: SkillTest, ChaosBagPull, EnemyEngagement, ActionsPerTurn budget, ScenarioProgress
- Core gaps: none; the scripted "villain side" runs through moment/event hooks and needs no real player input, since its choices are fixed by card text
- Fit: framework; needs a skill-test rule type and scripted-opponent conventions, no core change
- Confidence: high; played it, rules well documented

## Marvel Champions: The Card Game (2019, Fantasy Flight Games) — live
- Players: asym
- Turns: full; each hero takes a full turn (play cards, one scheme/attack/thwart/defend, one form change) then a scripted villain phase acts
- Resources: pitch; discard a card from hand for the resource icons printed on it to pay for another card
- Life: counter; hero HP counter, defeated at 0; the game also ends when the main scheme's threat reaches its limit
- Combat: other; attacking, thwarting and defending compare printed stats against villain/scheme values, not a paired duel
- Responses: windows; interrupt/response abilities trigger off named events, no shared stack
- Board: row; a hero's allies, supports and upgrades sit in an unordered play area
- Card kinds: heroes (hero identity), units (ally), permanents (support), attachments (upgrade), spells (event); the villain's scripted scheme/villain cards fit none
- Hook: A hero card flips between two faces (hero and alter-ego), each legal to play only its own half of the card pool, swapping the player's whole toolkit mid-game.
- Libraries sufficient: mostly; heroes (two-faced card), attachments, permanents, spells and resources.PitchFromHand all map directly
- Missing rules: FormFlip (identity swap changing legal plays), VillainScriptedPhase, SchemeThreat (a shared, race-like doom counter)
- Core gaps: none; villain behaviour is scripted through hooks, no real second player needed
- Fit: framework; needs a form-flip rule and scripted-villain conventions
- Confidence: high; well documented, close FFG-engine sibling of Arkham Horror LCG

## Warhammer 40,000: Conquest (2014, Fantasy Flight Games) — dead
- Players: 2
- Turns: full; a fixed phase sequence each round (Order, Planning, Deployment, Command, Battle) ends the round
- Resources: other; each player gets a fixed resource allotment each round, not accumulated or grown to a cap
- Life: race; win by claiming three planets sharing a symbol; no shared life counter is damaged
- Combat: compare-stat; players compare total command icons on units at each planet to win a "command struggle" there, rather than pairing attackers with defenders
- Responses: windows; interrupt/reaction timing after named steps, no shared stack
- Board: lanes; three simultaneously active planet cards are the battlefields units deploy into
- Card kinds: units, permanents (HQ support card), spells (event), heroes (Warlord, a unique always-in-play unit)
- Hook: Victory is scored by contesting three simultaneously active planets (lanes) for a matching resource symbol, not by reducing an opponent's life total.
- Libraries sufficient: mostly; combat's Lanes rule, units, permanents and heroes map; HQ is an ordinary off-lane Zone
- Missing rules: CommandStruggle (icon-total comparison per lane awarding control), PlanetSymbolVictory (race to n matching symbols), FlatPerRoundResource
- Core gaps: none
- Fit: framework; needs new win-condition and lane-scoring rule types
- Confidence: medium; rulebook and summaries fetched, not played

## Doomtown: Reloaded (2014, Alderac Entertainment Group) — live
- Players: 2-4
- Turns: full; Sunrise, High Noon, Gamblin', and Sundown phases each round, ending after Sundown
- Resources: other; Ghost Rock is produced each round by deeds a player controls and spent on cards/actions, not drawn or pitched from hand
- Life: race; a player wins by controlling deeds whose Control Point value reaches a target; also loses if unable to draw a card
- Combat: other; a Shootout is called at a location, dudes are dragged in, then resolved by comparing poker hands drawn from each side's own deck (lowest hand wins), the loser taking casualties equal to the rank difference
- Responses: windows; "during shootout" actions and hexes trigger at fixed moments within a shootout's rounds
- Board: none; dudes and goods sit in an unordered player area, grouped only by the location they occupy
- Card kinds: units (dude), permanents (deed), attachments (goods), spells (action/event), heroes (Outfit, an always-in-play identity)
- Hook: Fights ("shootouts") are resolved by drawing a poker hand from your own deck and comparing rank (lowball), so a player's deck is also their combat-randomness source.
- Libraries sufficient: mostly; units, permanents, heroes and common's draw/search map; the poker-hand duel is genuinely new
- Missing rules: PokerHandDuel, ShootoutZone (a temporary sub-group drawn from both sides), ProductionFromPermanents, ControlPointVictory
- Core gaps: none
- Fit: framework; the poker-hand resolution is a self-contained new combat rule, not a core change
- Confidence: medium; rulebook and summaries fetched, not played extensively

## Legend of the Five Rings: The Card Game (2017, Fantasy Flight Games) — dead
- Players: 2
- Turns: full; Dynasty, Draw, Conflict and Fate phases each round; the Conflict phase ends when both players pass on declaring conflicts
- Resources: growing; Fate is placed on cards from a shared pool that regrows each round from province values
- Life: counter+race; losing all four provinces (broken) loses the game; reaching 25 honor is an outright win, 0 honor an outright loss
- Combat: compare-stat; a conflict totals the military or political skill of all participating characters on each side, no paired duels
- Responses: windows; interrupt/reaction/pursuit abilities fire at named trigger points, no shared stack
- Board: row; a player's characters and their four face-down provinces are separate unordered rows
- Card kinds: units (character, with dual military/political skill instead of one power stat), permanents (holding), spells (event), attachments; Province (a face-down, breakable life-segment permanent) fits none
- Hook: Life is four individually-breakable Provinces, each hiding a card that resolves when attacked, so life loss and card advantage are the same event.
- Libraries sufficient: mostly; permanents, attachments and spells map; characters' two skills and provinces are new
- Missing rules: Province (record type), DualSkillCharacter (military/political, chosen by conflict type), HonorBid, ConflictDeclaration
- Core gaps: other: a card with two independent combat-relevant stats, one selected per instance of combat, isn't expressible with UnitCard's single `power`
- Fit: framework; province and honor mechanics are new records/rules, no change to what the core can represent
- Confidence: high; played it, rules well known

## Epic Card Game (2015, White Wizard Games) — live
- Players: 2-4
- Turns: full; each player's turn is a main phase then a battle phase, ending when they pass to the next player
- Resources: growing; gain 1 gold each turn, refilled rather than accumulated, spent on at most one gold-cost card per turn
- Life: counter; 30 health, eliminated at 0
- Combat: defender-blocks; the attacker commits champions, the defender assigns blockers or takes the unblocked damage to health
- Responses: windows; cards with the Ambush keyword may be played during the opponent's turn, in response to a trigger
- Board: row; champions sit in an unordered play area per player
- Card kinds: units (champion), spells (action); no hero/permanent/attachment card kind is used
- Hook: The base game supplies one shared 120-card pool split evenly between players, so there is no deckbuilding at all, only sealed play from a fixed card list.
- Libraries sufficient: mostly; units, spells, common, and responses.Ambush (already in the catalogue) map directly; resources.GrowingCounter with a per-turn refill covers gold
- Missing rules: none
- Core gaps: none
- Fit: libraries; a straightforward attacker/defender combat game already covered by the catalogue
- Confidence: medium; rulebook fetched, not played

## Ashes Reborn (2015 / 2020, Plaid Hat Games) — live
- Players: 2
- Turns: alternating; players alternate single actions until both pass in a row, ending the round
- Resources: other; a shared pool of six-sided dice is rolled each round, and a die is exhausted for the symbol effect it shows to pay costs
- Life: counter; each Phoenixborn has a life total, reduced by wounds, destroyed at 0
- Combat: defender-blocks; an attack targets the Phoenixborn or an ally, the defender may assign a defending ally to block and both exhaust
- Responses: windows; reaction abilities and "when attacked" triggers fire at fixed moments, no shared stack
- Board: row; allies sit in an unordered play area per player
- Card kinds: heroes (Phoenixborn), units (ally), spells (action/reaction), attachments (alteration)
- Hook: Costs are paid by exhausting dice from a shared, randomly-rolled pool rather than spending or discarding cards, so the resource itself carries randomness every round.
- Libraries sufficient: mostly; heroes, units, spells, attachments and turns.AlternatingActions map; the dice-pool resource is new
- Missing rules: DicePoolResource (a rolled, typed, exhaustible currency; core's `roll` operation can back it)
- Core gaps: none
- Fit: framework; needs a new ResourceRule for a rolled dice pool
- Confidence: medium; rulebook and previews fetched, general recall

## Mage Wars (2012, Arcane Wonders) — live
- Players: 2
- Turns: alternating; within a round's Action Phase, players alternate single quick/full actions until both pass
- Resources: growing; mana grows each round by a mage's channeling stat
- Life: counter; each mage has life points, loses at 0
- Combat: other; creatures (including mages) occupy squares on a shared grid board and must be adjacent or in range to attack, damage reducing life directly
- Responses: none; effects mostly resolve as taken, without a formal window or stack
- Board: grid; a mage-vs-mage battlefield of squares with movement and line of sight
- Card kinds: heroes (mage), units (creature), spells (attack/incantation), permanents (conjuration/enchantment), attachments (equipment)
- Hook: There is no deck or draw step at all: each mage pre-selects a fixed 120-point "spellbook" of known cards before the game and casts straight from it.
- Libraries sufficient: no; movement, adjacency, range and line-of-sight on a grid, and a no-draw known-spellbook pool, are both absent
- Missing rules: GridMovement, AdjacencyRange, LineOfSight, KnownSpellbook (no hand/draw, cards pre-chosen and visible)
- Core gaps: other: `position` is a single int per object, not 2D coordinates, so range and line-of-sight over a grid with terrain cannot be expressed without a library convention or a core extension
- Fit: framework; likely needs a core-adjacent addition for real 2D position, flagged as a risk rather than a certainty
- Confidence: medium; rulebook fetched, general recall, not played extensively

## Summoner Wars (2011 / 2021, Plaid Hat Games) — live
- Players: 2
- Turns: full; each turn a player gains magic, then plays units, moves and attacks, ending by passing to the opponent
- Resources: other; a separate "Magic Pile" of cards is spent one at a time (discarded), replenished by moving cards over from the main deck
- Life: other; no shared life pool; a player loses when their Summoner (a unit) is destroyed, tracked as that unit's own health
- Combat: attacker-chooses; the attacker picks an adjacent or ranged target and rolls dice to score hits against its defense, removing life
- Responses: none; most cards resolve immediately when played, no reaction window
- Board: grid; an 8x6 grid where units and walls occupy squares, units summoned only adjacent to a controlled wall
- Card kinds: heroes (Summoner), units (common/champion), permanents (wall), spells (event)
- Hook: Units may only be summoned adjacent to a wall you control, so board geometry gates deployment, not cost alone.
- Libraries sufficient: no; grid movement/adjacency and dice-resolved combat are absent, as is a placement-adjacent-to-X summoning rule
- Missing rules: GridMovement, AdjacencyRange, DiceCombatResolution, SummonAdjacentTo, DualPileResource
- Core gaps: other: the same grid-position limitation as Mage Wars (int position, no 2D coordinate/adjacency primitive)
- Fit: framework; grid/adjacency gap shared with other grid games, otherwise ordinary units/heroes/permanents
- Confidence: medium; official rules summary fetched, general recall

## Hex: Shards of Fate (2013, Cryptozoic Entertainment) — digital-dead
- Players: 2
- Turns: full; Draw, Main, Battle and end steps each turn, ending by passing to the opponent
- Resources: resource-cards; Resource cards played from hand provide generic resource points, a charge, and shard-type threshold
- Life: counter; each player has a hero with a health total, defeated at 0
- Combat: defender-blocks; the attacker declares troops to attack the hero, the defender assigns blockers among their troops
- Responses: stack; instant-speed cards and triggered abilities may respond to anything, resolving last-in-first-out
- Board: row; troops sit in an unordered play area per player
- Card kinds: heroes (Champion), units (troop), spells (action), permanents (constant/artifact), attachments (equipment)
- Hook: A card's colour requirement ("threshold") is met by shard-type resources you control, separate from the generic resource points spent to pay its cost.
- Libraries sufficient: mostly; heroes, units, spells, permanents, attachments and responses.Stack map directly
- Missing rules: ThresholdGate (a colour requirement checked against resources controlled, not consumed, gating legality to play a card)
- Core gaps: none
- Fit: framework; needs one new resource-policy-like rule for threshold gating
- Confidence: medium; official summaries fetched, general recall of this MTG-adjacent design, never played on paper (digital-only)
