# Aluxes (AL1): the second folklore deck

The second deck of the folklore re-theme (see [folk-creatures.md](folk-creatures.md) and
[designing-good-deck.md](designing-good-deck.md)): the aluxes, the little guardians of the Maya cornfield. It is a
starter deck, one every player has, next to the Domowiki and the Pari.

Status: released (2026-11), data in `content/2026/11/aluxes/set.json`. Last updated 2026-09-26.

## The creature

An **alux** (Yucatec Maya; plural *aluxo'ob*, in Spanish *aluxes*) is a knee-high spirit of the Yucatán Peninsula:
Yucatán, Quintana Roo and Campeche in Mexico, Belize and Guatemala. The set's `lore` holds the facts the game can show
with the deck:

- An alux is knee-high and looks like a tiny Maya farmer: white cotton clothes, a straw hat and huarache sandals.
- A farmer builds a doll-sized stone house, the *kahtal alux*, at the edge of his cornfield, and an alux comes to live
  in it.
- For seven years the alux helps the corn grow, calls the rain, and walks the field at night, whistling to scare off
  thieves and animals.
- After the seven years the farmer must seal the little house's door, or the alux runs wild and turns to tricks.
- The *j-meen*, the Maya healer, shapes an alux from clay, prays over it for weeks until it comes to life, and sets it
  in its house or under the oldest tree on the land, often a great ceiba.
- Aluxes are usually invisible. People know one is near from a shadow, a gentle breeze, giggles in the dark, or
  footprints the size of a bird's.
- They love offerings: *saká* (a sweet corn drink), honey, tortillas, fruit and sweets, left in the little house.
- Their tricks are small: putting out fires, tossing pots and pans, hiding tools, tossing pebbles at your feet, and
  leading walkers in circles in the *monte*, the jungle.
- People who met one say he was dressed like a farmer, with a slingshot in his sash and a little dog trotting behind.
- An alux may stop a traveller on the road and ask for a small offering. Give it, and he brings good luck.
- Aluxes guard cenotes and old ruins too.
- Don't say their name out loud: it calls them.
- In the 1990s a bridge near Cancún fell down three times while it was being built. A Maya healer said the aluxes were
  angry, so the builders made them a little house, and the bridge stood.
- Builders in Yucatán still hold a ceremony for the aluxes before breaking ground, and hotels keep alux houses on
  their grounds.

Sources: [Wikipedia, Alux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alux); [Yucatán Today, A house for an
alux](https://yucatantoday.com/en/blog/a-house-for-an-alux); [Mayan.org, The Aluxes](https://mayan.org/myths/aluxes/);
[Ancient Origins, Aluxes](https://www.ancient-origins.net/myths-legends-americas/aluxes-0016592); [Na'atik, Maya
Aluxes](https://naatikmexico.org/blog/maya-aluxes-mysterious-elven-guardians-of-the-yucatn-jungle); [Milenio, Leyenda
de los aluxes](https://www.milenio.com/estados/leyenda-de-los-aluxes-en-yucatan-historia).

Care: a living, openly shared belief. The set shows aluxes as small spirits, never as caricatures of Maya people, and
no Maya gods appear on any card. A Maya or Yucatecan reviewer would still help.

## How it plays

The deck is the Starter Box's Orchard Guard, card for card: the same costs, stats, keywords and abilities, re-themed.
Orchard Guard is the patient deck (Guardians, healing, units that grow each round), and that is the alux's job: guard
the milpa, call the rain, and let the corn grow.

- **Rain-Fed** replaces Ripen: at the start of each round the unit gets +1/+1, up to +2/+2. Badge: 🌽. Same counter
  as Ripen, defined in the set's `mechanics`; no engine code.
- Guardians are aluxes on patrol; heals are the rain; the Toy is the little house itself.
- The deck uses the Starter Box's Garden slots (Orchard Guard has no Garden Snail), so the set `requires` SB1, but
  every card in the deck is an Aluxes card of its own.

## Hero: Aluxito, Keeper of the Milpa (AL1-H01, Legendary)

| Side | Name | Power | Text |
|---|---|---|---|
| first | Aluxito, Keeper of the Milpa | – | Exhaust: Heal 2 from a unit you control. Awaken: You have 6 or fewer Lives. |
| Awakened | Aluxito, Caller of the Rain | 3 | Exhaust: Heal 3 from a unit you control. It gains Guardian this round. |

*Aluxito* is the Yucatecan Spanish diminutive, "little alux", the fond word people use for them. He is the smallest
alux of all, in a hat a size too big, with the little dog the stories say walks behind an alux. He Awakens when the
field is under threat (6 or fewer Lives) and then calls the rain from the roof of his house. Legendary, and the only
gold frame in the deck; painted like every other card.

## Lore in the cards

Every card is a real belief from the sources above:

- **Kahtal Alux, the Little House**: the doll-sized house; whoever lives in it guards the corn for seven years.
- **Clay Alux, Fresh from the J-meen**: the healer shapes it from clay; it grows into life (Rain-Fed).
- **Corn-Tending Alux**: tends the crops overnight (Rain-Fed).
- **Night Patrol Alux**, **Slingshot Alux**, **The Alux's Dog**: the alux on his rounds, as witnesses described him.
- **A Whistle in the Dark**: the whistle that turns thieves and animals back (cancels an attack).
- **Saká-Bearing Alux**, **Honey and Tortillas**, **A Sweet on the Doorstep**: the offerings.
- **Tool-Hiding Alux**, **Pots and Pans Flung**, **Lost in the Monte**: the tricks.
- **Alux of the Old Stones**, **Alux of the Ceiba Roots**, **The Alux of the Sacred Cenote**: where they live.
- **The Roadside Alux**: asks travellers for an offering and pays in luck (heal, then draw).
- **The Bridge Alux of Cancún**: the bridge that fell three times (Guardian, Tough 1).
- **Toh, the Cenote Bird**: the turquoise-browed motmot that nests in cenote walls (Hello: draw a card).

## Art

**Since 2026-09-27: an ancient Maya wall painting.** The owner restyled the deck after two references of real Maya
murals (a feast scene, and a procession of figures in dotted, fringed robes): old art rather than game art, like the
Jiaoren and the Pari. Every card looks like a photograph of a thousand-year-old mural: flat mineral colours (red-orange,
terracotta, ochre, cream, black, touches of Maya blue and jade) on weathered lime plaster, bold red-brown outlines,
figures in profile with the classic Maya face, dotted and fringed cloth, faded and flaking paint. An alux is a stocky,
child-sized figure in a round straw hat, a short white cotton tunic with a dotted sash, and sandals. The references show
Maya nobles; the prompts use only their style, never their figures, and no gods, rulers, idols or temples are drawn.
The first four cards were a prototype the owner approved (Draft 2 of two) before the whole deck was redrawn.

The first look (2026-09-26 to 2026-09-27), carved jade-green figures in a misty jungle after a stylized digital render,
was retired: it read like a video game, and the owner wants the decks to look like their culture's own old art.

Direction and one subject per card in `art/prompts.json`; drawn with
`python tools/generate_art.py --set al1 --model gpt-image-2 --quality high --jobs 1 --reference <the owner's image>`
(one at a time; the rate limit refuses anything faster) and composed with `python tools/compose_cards.py --set al1`.

## Everyone has it

The set is `"starter": true`, so the deck is in every player's Collection from the start, like the Domowiki and the
Pari (the owner's call, 2026-09-26: the deck wasn't showing in the Collection while it was only a free Store deck,
which needs a tester account). Making it a Store deck later is one line in the set.

## What it needs built

Nothing: the rules are Orchard Guard's, and Rain-Fed is data. Gameplay closer to the folklore (the seven-year seal,
the offering a roadside alux asks for) can come later, once the deck has been played.
