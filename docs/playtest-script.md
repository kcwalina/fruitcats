# Watching someone learn Fruitcats

A one-page script for sitting with a new player. It exists because "the tutorial isn't good enough" is
not something you can act on, and "four out of five people planted their cheapest cards" is.

Use it with **someone who has never played a card game**. It takes about fifteen minutes.

## Before they arrive

- Open the game in a **private window** (the game remembers whether this browser has played before, and
  a first visit is a different experience).
- Have a way to record the screen, or a second person to take notes.
- Do not explain anything. If they ask you a rules question, say *"what do you think?"* and write the
  question down — every question you answer is a question the game failed to.

## What to say

> I'm testing the game, not you. Please think out loud — say what you're looking at, what you expect
> to happen, and when you're confused. If you get stuck, stay stuck for a bit; that's the useful part.

## The five tasks

Let them work. Note the time and what they tried.

1. **Start a game.** (Do they pick the Tutorial on the home screen, or go straight to Solo?)
2. **Put a cat on the table.**
3. **Say what the number in the corner of a card means.**
4. **Attack the other cat.**
5. **Say who moves next, and why.**

## What to write down

For each task: how long it took, what they tried first, and every question they asked out loud.

Then tick anything you see. These are the six confusions real testers have already hit — if they come
up again, the fix for that concept did not work:

- [ ] Planted a card expecting it to be played ("why did my cat go in the tray?")
- [ ] Did not know cards cost anything
- [ ] Expected to take several actions in a row
- [ ] Could not say what the mulligan was for
- [ ] Asked what a keyword meant (Zest, Zoomies, Guardian…) and did not find the answer
- [ ] Thought resting units applied only to the opponent

And three signals about the walkthrough itself:

- [ ] Closed a balloon in under a second or two (they are not reading it)
- [ ] Skipped the walkthrough
- [ ] Asked a question the balloon on screen had just answered (it is in the wrong place, or too long)

## Afterwards

Ask them, in this order, and write the answers verbatim:

1. What were you trying to do?
2. Those two cards you buried at the start — what were they for?
3. You played a cat and could not attack with it. Why?
4. You just attacked. What happens now?

A player who can answer all four has learned the game. One who cannot tells you exactly which concept to
fix next.

## The numbers the game already keeps

The game counts, **in that browser only and nowhere else**, how far the walkthrough got and how many
games were finished. Open the **Rules** panel in game and read the last line — something like
*"On this device: 2 games finished · walkthrough started 1×, last step 'attack'"*. It is the quickest way
to see where someone stopped, and it is shown to the player rather than sent anywhere.
