# Playtest feedback: the night of 2026-10-01

This was the first night under the new rules, Folkborn 0.4. Rounds are now a Muster, where both players build in
secret, and a Clash that plays itself. This review covers every report from the nightly run
`nightly-20261001-070030`, not only the win rates:

- **187 LLM games:**
  - `llm-playtest-20261001-105816`: 109 games with the five starters.
  - `llm-playtest-20261001-093526`: 78 games with two custom decks, a random Dziadziuś deck and Jiaoren with 8 cards
    swapped. The deck library was empty, so there were no library decks.
- **The bot gauntlet:** `balance-20261001-070030`, 258,000 games.

The LLM player is gpt-oss:20b. It won 7 of its 187 games (4%): 2% with the starters and 6% with the custom decks.
The night before, under the old rules, it won 6%.

Its reports hold 417 things it found confusing, 260 things it found unfair, and many mentions of cards as suspect.
They were checked in two ways:

- **Replaying the games.** All 187 games were replayed exactly with the engine, matching the LLM's choices one by one.
  Every replay ended with the same winner in the same round. The transcripts record only the LLM's choices, so the
  real Clash logs come from these replays.
- **Bot games.** Each suspect card was removed from its deck, about 3,200 games per card. The Aluxes–Pari pairing got
  about 30 more experiments, at 600 to 1,500 games each.

## How the LLM played: it destroyed its own board

The new rules gave the LLM two bad habits. Neither is a rule problem.

| Habit | Count |
|---|---|
| Offered a unit from its board for 1 Offering | 267 times |
| … of those, with room on the board left (pure loss) | 131 |
| Moves and swaps between lanes | 2,353, against 2,461 cards played |
| Rounds with 4 or more moves | 244 of 671 rounds with moves |
| Fell back to the bot (no usable answer) | 265 of about 9,000 moves |

A unit costs 1 to 8 Offerings and fights in every Clash for the rest of the game. Offering it for 1 throws that away.
In game 1, the LLM played Keeper of the Door for 3 and offered it for 1 on the next move. The shuffling between lanes
is harmless but wastes its moves. In one round it moved Keeper of the Door five times and ended where it started.

Two new strategy tips:

- Never offer a unit from your board just for the Offering. Offer one only when your Level is full and you have a
  better unit to put there.
- Only where your units stand when you are Ready matters: move each unit once.

The number to watch tomorrow is "Offer your unit" with room left, in the transcripts.

### Why Pari beats Aluxes: one cheap Guardian

It isn't Pari's Sneaky units. It is **Lookout on Mount Qāf** (cost 2, 1/4, Guardian), Pari's only Guardian. Aluxes
have no Sneaky unit, so every Alux has to hit the Guardian first. In one sample Clash, six Aluxes put 39 damage into
one 1/4 Lookout in the first bout while Pari's units hit freely. Per game, Lookout takes 2.6 times more hits than any
other unit.

| Experiment (paired games against Aluxes) | Pari's win rate against Aluxes |
|---|---|
| As it is | 64.5% |
| Without Lookout on Mount Qāf | 29.7% |
| Without any other single Pari card | at most 5 points lower |

Lookout carries Pari in every pairing, so weakening it breaks Pari elsewhere:

### Aluxito: one ability carries the Aluxes deck

Five random decks with Aluxito (the Aluxes Hero) and Pari cards beat the starters 64–69%. The cause is Aluxito's
Awakened ability: "A unit you control gets +2 Rain-Fed". It works on any unit, and since units are never lost in a
Clash, the +2/+2 stays for the rest of the game.

| Pari's starter cards, led by | Overall against the starters |
|---|---|
| Parijan (as it is) | 52% |
| Aluxito | 81% |
| Aluxito, ability only on Rain-Fed units | 56% |

The Aluxes starter depends on the same ability. Without it, Aluxes falls from 47% to 23%, and limiting it to
Rain-Fed units gives the same drop. So it can't be fixed by trimming the ability alone. This is left for the owner
(below).

## Unchanged, and why

- **Offering a unit from the board:** the rules allow it on purpose, to make room. The tips now teach when.
- **Same-named units on both sides** ("Kikimora hits Kikimora"): about 6% of Clashes. The new "finds" line says whose
  unit an effect found. The hit lines still don't name the owner. Left for now.
- **Two copies of a card in hand show as two identical choices** to the LLM. Harmless, but it made 5 replays pick the
  other copy's id. Left for now.

## For the owner

**Aluxito's Awakened ability is too strong outside its own deck, and the Aluxes deck rests on it.** The options:

1. **Leave it.** The starters are balanced, but any deck with Aluxito and strong units from another family will beat
   them. Players who mix families will find this quickly.
2. **Limit it to Aluxes cards, or to Rain-Fed units, and rebuild the Aluxes deck around stronger Aluxes cards.** This
   keeps Aluxito's identity (it grows Aluxes) but needs a balance pass on the whole deck, since Aluxes would fall to
   about 23% without other changes.
3. **Make the +2 Rain-Fed last only this round,** as the other Heroes' boosts do. This is simpler, but it changes
   what Aluxito is ("grows the corn for good").

Option 2 is the recommendation. It is a design decision, so nothing was changed tonight.
