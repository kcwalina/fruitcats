## Why the LLM did worse: yesterday's strategy tips

The LLM won 6% last night, against 11% the night before. The bots judge every LLM move. By that judgment, the LLM
passed while it still had a useful move 1.3 times a game, against 0.45 the night before, in the same starter games.

| | 2026-09-29 | 2026-09-30 |
|---|---|---|
| LLM won | 11% | 6% |
| Passes a game | 2.7 | 4.7 |
| Passed with a useful move left, a game | 0.45 | 1.32 |
| … when the best move it skipped was an attack | 0.29 | 0.77 |
| … when it held no Ambush card | 0.40 | 1.11 |

The cause is yesterday's new tips, and the way the model read the Lantern rules:

- "End your turn with enough ready Offerings to pay for an Ambush" made it keep Offerings back, even without an
  Ambush card in hand. It explained passes with "keeping my Offerings for next round", but Offerings don't carry
  over.
- "Check the trade still works if it is 2 stronger" made it skip attacks, including hits on the Hero, which a +2
  Power Ambush can't stop.
- It confused holding the Lantern (acting first) with taking it ("I hold the Lantern, so I may only pass").

The tips now say to attack every round, that a hit on the Hero is safe from a +2 Power Ambush, to keep only one
Offering for an Ambush card, and that Offerings don't carry over. The rules the LLM reads now say that holding the
Lantern never limits you; only taking it does.

The model also wasted its own +2 Power Ambushes. It played them 330 times, and only 7 were on a unit in the fight;
the bots' 131 all were. The tips now say where that Ambush helps.

Tomorrow's nightly will show whether this worked: the number to watch is "passes with a useful move left" in the
LLM report.

## The Lantern

The engine follows the rules. Scans of all 3,054 rounds in the 471 games found:

- no round in which the Lantern was taken twice;
- no player who acted, other than Ambushing or playing a Lucky Candle, after taking it;
- the right player acting first in every round.

The confusion came from four places:

| Question | Reports | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| "Both players took the Lantern in one round" | about 33 | Misreading. A holder who keeps the Lantern was logged as "takes the Lantern" again. The log now says "keeps", and each round starts with "LLM holds the Lantern and acts first." |
| Taking it while already holding it | about 54 | Allowed (rule 300.5). The Quick Rules now say so too. |
| "After taking it I still Ambushed" | about 110 | Allowed (rule 300.4). Already in the label and the LLM's rules. |
| "My opponent kept acting after I took it" | about 15 | Correct: they act until they pass. The Quick Rules and rulebook 6.2 now say so. |

### Old One of the Waterfall

Without it, Hui Hai falls from 51% to 37% and would be the weakest starter by far. The number overstates it: the
test replaces a 7-cost finisher with a 1-cost 2/1, so the deck loses its top end. Elder of the South Sea and
Quanxian did the same for Jiaoren yesterday (−5.9 and −4.8). Hui Hai is at 52%, inside the band, so it stays as it
is. It is worth watching: a deck that leans this hard on one card plays the same way every game.

## Unchanged, and why

- **"Taking the Lantern is too punitive":** the design. Going first next round is paid for with this round.
- **"The attacker stays exhausted after a cancelled attack":** the design, and what A Whistle in the Dark says.
- **The log line for a Hero's ability doesn't say what it did** ("uses their ability on Pebble-Thrower"): a small gap;
  the following lines show the effect. Left for now.
- **Two units with the same name in one fight** ("Lookout on Mount Qāf takes 1, Lookout on Mount Qāf takes 1"):
  rare. Left for now.
