156 games, prioritised by notability (sales, player base, longevity, competitive scene) and then by
structural variety. Compiled from the Wikipedia "List of collectible card games", BoardGameGeek's
Collectible Components category, TCGplayer's game categories, Kickstarter, Fantasy Flight's LCG
line, and indietcg.net / cardgamer.com for indie titles. Status: live (physical, in print/supported),
dead (out of print/discontinued), digital-live, digital-dead, kickstarter (crowdfunding or
post-campaign pre-wide-release).

## Not included

- **Dominion, Star Realms, Hero Realms, Ascension** — pure deck-building board games; no persistent collectible card pool traded between players.
- **Slay the Spire, Monster Train, Inscryption** — roguelike deckbuilders, single-player-first design, not card-vs-card TCGs.
- **Sorare** — fantasy-sports NFT game scored on real athlete stats, not a card-vs-card battle game.
- **UNO** — a fixed, non-collectible retail card game; no boosters, no deckbuilding.
- **Vampire: The Masquerade – Rivals, Mystic Vale** — deckbuilders using a shared card pool, not packs owned/traded by players.
- **Legendary: A Marvel Deck Building Game, DC Deck-Building Game** — deckbuilders, excluded for the same reason.
- **Sentinels of the Multiverse** — cooperative fixed-deck card game; no deckbuilding or collectible packs.
- **Magic: The Gathering Arena, Yu-Gi-Oh! Master Duel** — digital clients of already-listed paper games, not separate designs.
- **Munchkin CCG, Warhammer 40,000: Conquest expansions, Legend of the Burning Sands** — counted as part of their base game's row rather than separately.
- **StarCraft board/card products** — Blizzard's StarCraft tie-ins are board games or minis games, not CCGs.
- Dozens of 1990s one-set-and-gone licensed CCGs from the Wikipedia list (e.g. Xena, Monster Rancher, Fastbreak) were passed over in favor of higher-notability or more structurally distinct entries, to keep the survey at ~150 rather than the full 500+ catalogued there.
