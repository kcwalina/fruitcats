# Playtest feedback: the night of 2026-09-29

This covers every report from the nightly run `nightly-20260929-070030`, not only the win rates:

- 426 LLM games: `llm-playtest-20260929-093849` with the five starters, and `llm-playtest-20260929-074056` with
  custom decks.
- The bot gauntlet: `balance-20260929-070030`, 258,000 games.

The LLM player is gpt-oss:20b. It won 11% of its games.

Its 426 reports hold 921 things it found confusing, 555 things it found unfair, and 1,681 mentions of a card as
suspect. Each was checked in one of two ways:

- **Replaying the game.** Every one of the 426 games was replayed exactly with the engine, matching the LLM's
  choices one by one, so each claim was compared with the real game log.
- **Bot games.** For the suspect cards, bot games were played with each card removed or changed.

Each game's transcript can now be fetched from PC2024 (`POST /runs/game {id, n}` on the playtester), so the next
review can read the whole reports too.

## The Lantern

The Lantern was the biggest source of confusion. The questions were:

- What may I still do after taking it?
- Can it be taken twice in a round?
- What happens when nobody takes it?
- Why did the other player keep acting?

The engine follows the rulebook exactly (rules 300.4, 300.5 and 300.6). Across all 426 replayed games:

- The player who took the Lantern afterwards only Ambushed (59 times), played a Lucky Candle (70 times), declined
  Ambushes or discarded. Never anything else.
- Nobody took it twice in one round.
- All 2,574 changes of round passed the Lantern correctly.

The short rules left out the details that caused the questions. Three texts now say that the taker may still Ambush
(and play a Lucky Candle), and that when nobody takes the Lantern it goes to the other player:

- the in-game Quick Rules;
- the Take the Lantern choice;
- the LLM's rules.

The LLM's rules also say that you may take the Lantern while you already hold it, to keep it, and that your
opponent keeps acting until they pass.

"Taking the Lantern is too punitive" (a few reports) is the design: going first next round is paid for with this
round. In bot games, whoever holds the Lantern first wins 50%, so it is balanced.

## Ambush

The questions were who pays for an Ambush, whether it uses your turn, and what a cancelled attack does. The
Quick Rules and the LLM's rules now answer all three:

- The Ambush is paid from your own ready Offerings.
- It doesn't use your turn.
- A cancelled attack deals no damage either way, and the attacker stays exhausted.

Only A Whistle in the Dark cancels attacks. The engine does this correctly.

The "unfair" reports are almost all the same card type. Warm Hand in the Night, Zangwar, A Sweet on the Doorstep,
Tears of Gratitude and Small but Strong are each a 1-cost "+2 Power this round" Ambush, 3 copies in each of the
five decks. That accounts for about 150 suspect-card mentions.

In bot games, each deck wins more with that card replaced by a plain 1-cost 2/1:

| Deck | Change with the card removed |
|---|---|
| Aluxes | +1.2 points |
| Domowiki | +1.9 points |
| Jiaoren | +2.1 points |
| Hui Hai | +2.4 points |
| Pari | +4.7 points |

The card isn't strong; the model just never plays around it. Its strategy tips now tell it three things:

- expect a unit to be 2 stronger while the opponent has an Offering ready;
- keep its own Ambush cards instead of offering them;
- keep an Offering ready to pay for one.

Many of the complaints were about the LLM's own cards (40 of Warm Hand's 51). The same card was often called
overpowered and useless in the same batch. Many specific claims were simply wrong, for example "The House Snake
costs 4" or "Dziadziuś used its ability twice in a round".

### What the bots flag: Elder of the South Sea, Dvorovoi, Quanxian

The nightly says decks win 23, 13 and 12 points more often in games where these cards are played. That number
overstates them. An 8-cost card is only played in long games the player was already surviving.

Removing them costs their decks −5.9, −2.3 and −4.8 points. They are strong, but no more than a good card should
be:

- Elder and Quanxian hold up Jiaoren, the weakest starter at 47% (40% against Domowiki).
- Every weakening that was tried takes Jiaoren under 45%.

So they stay. If Jiaoren changes, it needs a buff against Domowiki. The nightly's per-card number should be
replaced by something that doesn't reward late cards; that is a separate piece of work.

### The generated decks that beat the starters

Nine random decks beat the starters 62–75%. The Zhu'er decks among them lean on cheap Guardians and Dragon Silk,
not on Elder: the best one (75%) has no Elder at all. This is worth watching. No change yet.

### Pari

Pari wins 48%, inside the 45–55% band. For the bots, its 4–5 cost cards do worse than its cheap ones (removing
Markhor: +7.1, Zangwar: +4.7, Schaibar: +2.3). A later look could make Markhor cheaper or bigger. It isn't needed
now.

## Why the LLM loses so much with Aluxes (2 of 55)

It's the model, not the deck. Aluxes wins 49% for the bots. It is a slow deck that needs careful play: heals,
trades, Rain-Fed growth, and a Hero that Awakens late. With it, the model:

- blew out the fewest Candles of any deck (3.1 a game; Pari 5.0);
- offered away its Ambush cards, A Whistle in the Dark 29 times;
- aimed Pots and Pans Flung at its own units 6 times;
- healed units that had no damage.

The new strategy tips cover the last three.

## Unchanged, and why

- **"Losing a Candle for an empty deck is harsh":** it is the rule that ends long games; it rarely comes up.
- **"A new unit can't attack at once"** and **"A Hero can't attack before it Awakens":** both are the design.
  Swift and Awakening are what change them.
- **The Awaken log line** ("Aluxito Awakens as Aluxito, Caller of the Rain!"): it gives the full Awakened name.
  Left as is.
